## 1. Grade 4 ELA essential standards, by quarter

Code parts, from the 2023 Arkansas Academic Standards scheme: **FR** foundational reading, **PD** decoding, **PE** encoding, **F** fluency, **V** vocabulary, **RC** reading comprehension, **RL** literature, **RI** informational text, **RF** applies to reading generally, **L** language, **S** sentence structure, **W** writing, **P** production.

### Reading / vocabulary

| Standard | In plain words | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| **4.FR.6.F** | Read a passage out loud correctly, smoothly and with expression, at a speed that still lets you understand it, and fix your own mistakes as you go. | ● | ● | ● | ● |
| **4.FR.1.PD** | Read long words by spotting their Latin parts: the prefix at the front, the base in the middle, the suffix at the end, and the letter that joins them. | ● | ● | ● | ● |
| **4.FR.4.PE** | Spell long words by putting those same Latin parts back together, including prefixes that change shape next to the base (*in-* → *im-possible*, *ad-* → *ac-cept*). | | ● | ● | ● |
| **4.V.2** | Work out what a new word means from the rest of the sentence — the cause-and-effect or the comparison the writer put around it. | ● | ● | ● | ● |
| **4.RC.2.RF** | Answer questions about a text: the ones it says straight out *and* the ones you have to work out, pointing to the details you used. | ● | ● | ● | ● |
| **4.RC.9.RL** | Say what the lesson or big idea of a story is — the theme — not just what happened. | | ● | ● | (●) |
| **4.RC.3.RF** | Sum up a text of several paragraphs in your own words, keeping the key details that show what it was mainly about. | | | ● | ● |
| **4.RC.14.RI** | Explain how a writer backs up a point: which reasons and which pieces of evidence he used. | | | ● | ● |

(●) = reassessed in Q4 only if not yet proficient, per the FPS note.

### Writing / grammar

| Standard | In plain words | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| **4.L.14.S** | Write complex sentences: add a dependent clause with a joining word like *because, although, when, if, since*. | ● | | | |
| **4.W.4.P** | Write clearly so a reader can follow you — exact words, details that matter, ideas developed, Grade 4 spelling and punctuation. | ● | ● | ● | ● |

**Reading the shape of the year:** fluency, word parts, context clues and literal-plus-inferential questions run all year and are the daily bread. Theme arrives in Q2. Summarising and author's-evidence arrive in Q3 and are the hardest lift. Complex sentences are formally taught in Q1 and then assumed.

## 4. Grade 3 ELA essentials — where Nour's reading currently sits

This is the bridge. Everything in Grade 4 is the same skill one notch harder.

| Grade 3 standard | Plain | Grade 4 successor |
|---|---|---|
| 3.FR2.PD (all quarters) | Sound out multi-syllable base words (*neighbor, suitcase, fascinate, suggest*). | 4.FR.1.PD — same job, but by Latin prefix + base + suffix. |
| 3.FR.6.PE (Q1 in, reported Q2–Q4) | Spell two-syllable words with long-vowel teams (*complain, canteen*). | 4.FR.4.PE — spell by assembling Latin parts. |
| 3.FR.8.PE (Q4) | Spell using common Latin prefixes, bases and suffixes, inflectional and derivational. | 4.FR.4.PE directly. **This is the handoff point.** |
| 3.FR.11.F (all quarters) | Read aloud accurately, smoothly, with expression; self-correct. | 4.FR.6.F — same, faster text. |
| 3.V.2 (all quarters) | Use context clues to identify word meaning. | 4.V.2 — now *infer* meaning, with cause/effect and comparison clues. |
| 3.RC.8.RL (Q2) | Describe story elements: characters, setting, events, problem, solution. | 4.RC.9.RL — from *what happened* to *what it means* (theme). |
| 3.RC.14.RI (Q3) | Compare and contrast two texts on the same topic. | 4.RC.14.RI — how one author supports a point with reasons and evidence. |
| 3.RC.3.RF (Q4) | Summarise multi-paragraph texts with key details. | 4.RC.3.RF — identical wording, longer texts. |
| 3.RC.13.RI (Q4) | Explain how an author uses reasons and evidence. | 4.RC.14.RI — identical wording. |
| 3.L.17.S (Q1–Q2) | Combine independent clauses into **compound** sentences. | 4.L.14.S — **complex** sentences with dependent clauses. |
| 3.W.4.P | Clear, coherent writing with precise language and details. | 4.W.4.P — identical wording. |

**Practical read:** the two real jumps are (a) compound → complex sentences and (b) story elements → theme. Summary and author's-evidence are word-for-word the same standard in both grades, so Grade 3 material there is not remediation, it is the same target at an easier reading level.

## 6. App mapping

For each essential standard: which app item types exercise it, and whether the item type already exists in the rebuild plan. The current plan has **recognize** (meaning → word MCQ), **listen** (audio → word), **spell** (letter tiles / type from audio), **use** (cloze, pick-the-correct-sentence, pick-the-right-word-form), and **reading** (author prompts + inference + detail + retell).

| Standard | App item types | New? |
|---|---|---|
| **4.FR.1.PD** — decode by Latin parts | `word-part-meaning`: show a prefix, base or suffix and pick what it means (*re- = again / not / under / before*). `word-part-build`: build a word from prefix + base + suffix tiles to fit a meaning (*"someone who acts" → act + -or*). Also feeds `recognize` distractors: same-prefix words. | **NEW** ×2 |
| **4.FR.4.PE** — encode by Latin parts | `word-part-spell`: hear a word, then assemble it from part tiles rather than letters, including the shape-changing prefixes (*in- + possible → impossible*). Falls back to the existing `spell` when the word has no useful parts. | **NEW** ×1 |
| **4.V.2** — context clues | `context-clue`: read one or two sentences with a bold word never seen before, pick its meaning from 4 options; the sentence must contain the cause/effect or comparison clue. Distractors are near-misses, not random. Existing `use` (cloze) covers the reverse direction. | **NEW** ×1 |
| **4.RC.2.RF** — explicit + inferential questions | Existing reading questions, split and labelled: `reading-detail` (answer is a sentence in the text) and `reading-inference` (answer is not stated). Feedback highlights the sentence the answer came from. | existing |
| **4.RC.3.RF** — summarise | Existing `retell-pick`: after a passage, pick the best one-sentence summary out of 3 (one correct, one too narrow / a single detail, one wrong). | existing |
| **4.RC.9.RL** — theme of a story | `theme-pick`: after a narrative passage, pick the lesson from 4 options — one theme, one plot summary, one detail, one theme that fits a different story. Only offered on fiction passages. | **NEW** ×1 |
| **4.RC.14.RI** — author's reasons and evidence | `evidence-pick`: given a claim from the passage ("the author says the railroad was dangerous"), pick which sentence is the evidence for it. Pairs with the existing author prompts on informational passages. | **NEW** ×1 |
| **4.FR.6.F** — oral fluency | `read-aloud-timed`: start/stop a short passage, count words per minute, chart against the Hasbrouck & Tindal Grade 4 norms (94 fall → 133 spring). Repeated reading of the same passage 2–3 times. Listed in the plan as an optional "time my read" toggle; this promotes it to a scored, tracked item type. | **NEW** ×1 (promoted) |
| **4.L.14.S** — complex sentences | `sentence-combine`: show two short sentences, pick or build the joined version using *because / although / when / if / since*; a second form gives the joining word and asks which clause depends on which. | **NEW** ×1 |
| **4.W.4.P** — clear writing | No new item type. Served by `sentence-combine`, the existing `use` "own sentence" mode and `retell-pick`. Free writing stays out of scope — nothing here grades an essay. | existing |

**Science mapping.** Science is not a separate mode. It feeds the ELA work three ways:

1. **Informational reading passages** on the science unit that is running that week (`scienceUnitForWeek`), which is exactly where `evidence-pick` and cause/effect questions belong — 4.RC.14.RI and 4.RC.2.RF get real non-fiction to work on instead of invented topics.
2. **A seeded vocabulary list per unit** (12–15 words each, §3), used when the parent has not added a word list; these are also the tier-3 words the school is using that month.
3. **2–3 fact-check MCQ items after a science passage** (`science-fact`) — did the passage actually say this — which check comprehension without demanding science mastery.

**Weekly theme wiring.** `themeForWeek(date)` returns the Benchmark unit for the week; the reading generator uses its essential question as one of the two author prompts and pulls 3–5 of its theme words as the passage's glossed unknown words. `currentQuarter(date)` gates which standards the "At school now" strip shows, so theme-pick does not appear before Q2 and evidence-pick does not appear before Q3.

**Quarter gating (built 2026-08-22).** `questionPlan()` takes the current quarter and opens `theme` in Q2, `retell` and `evidence` in Q3, whatever the reading level is. Before this, those three question types were gated on reading level alone (>= 4, >= 5), which meant a child reading at level 1 would never meet the three standards FPS grades hardest — 4.RC.9.RL, 4.RC.3.RF and 4.RC.14.RI. The level thresholds still apply out of term.

### New item types, at a glance

1. `word-part-meaning` — pick what a prefix / base / suffix means (4.FR.1.PD)
2. `word-part-build` — build a word from part tiles (4.FR.1.PD)
3. `word-part-spell` — spell a word by assembling its Latin parts, incl. assimilated prefixes (4.FR.4.PE)
4. `context-clue` — pick the meaning of a bold word from the sentence around it (4.V.2)
5. `theme-pick` — pick the theme of a story (4.RC.9.RL)
6. `evidence-pick` — pick the sentence that is the author's evidence for a claim (4.RC.14.RI)
7. `read-aloud-timed` — timed read-aloud scored in WPM against grade norms (4.FR.6.F; promoted from an optional toggle)
8. `sentence-combine` — join two sentences with because / although / when (4.L.14.S, 4.W.4.P)
9. `science-fact` — 2–3 fact-check MCQs after a science passage (comprehension support)

Reused unchanged: `recognize`, `listen`, `spell`, `use` (cloze / pick-correct-sentence / word-form), `reading-detail`, `reading-inference`, `reading-author`, `retell-pick`.

## 7. Data file

`lib/curriculum.ts` holds all of the above as typed, pure data:

- `FPS_QUARTERS`, `currentQuarter(dateISO)`
- `ELA_STANDARDS` — code, plain wording, quarters, item types
- `READING_THEMES`, `themeForWeek(dateISO)` — 10 Benchmark units spread over the instructional weeks in order
- `LATIN_PARTS` — 31 Grade 3–4 prefixes, bases and suffixes with meanings and examples
- `SCIENCE_UNITS`, `scienceUnitForWeek(dateISO)`

Tests: `lib/__tests__/curriculum.test.ts`. Run with `npm test` (already wired to `tsx --test lib/__tests__/*.test.ts`), or a single file with `npx tsx --test lib/__tests__/curriculum.test.ts`.

**Caveats to keep in mind.** FPS unit plans are behind a school login, so unit-level pacing is inferred, not published. The science week counter runs straight through breaks, so week 36 lands in mid-April and the last weeks of the year stay on the engineering unit. Spring break is not in the public Year-at-a-Glance and is not modelled.
