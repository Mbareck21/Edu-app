# Pedagogy brief: home English app for a Grade 4 Arabic-L1 boy

Target: move reading, vocabulary and comprehension from Grade 3 to Grade 4 in ~12 weeks of daily 20-30 minute sessions. Everything below is what the research supports, with the numbers I would actually hard-code.

## 1. Vocabulary

**1. Budget 8-12 spaced meaningful encounters before calling a word "known."** Webb (2007) compared 1, 3, 7 and 10 encounters and found every increase added something, with 10 still short of full word knowledge. One encounter buys almost nothing (5-15% recall). Ten encounters in informative context is the realistic working target. Webb, *Applied Linguistics* 28(1), 2007, https://academic.oup.com/applij/article-abstract/28/1/46/174744

**2. Space those encounters, and stretch the gaps.** Cepeda et al. (2006) synthesised 184 articles: the best gap between study sessions is roughly 10-20% of how long you want the word to stick. For a 3-month goal that means final gaps of one to two weeks, not one day. Nakata (2015) found a small but real edge for expanding gaps over equal gaps in L2 vocabulary. Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer, *Psychological Bulletin*, 2006, https://augmentingcognition.com/assets/Cepeda2006.pdf and Nakata, *SSLA* 37, 2015, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/effects-of-expanding-and-equal-spacing-on-second-language-vocabulary-learning/D1D796306985C52F9BE7A1200AC50DB9

**3. Keep testing after the first correct answer. Do not switch to re-showing.** Karpicke & Roediger (2008) showed dropping items once they are answered correctly wrecks retention, while continued retrieval doubled one-week recall. This is the single most important design rule in the app. Karpicke & Roediger, *Science* 319, 2008, summary at https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2007/2007_Karpicke_Roediger_JML.pdf

**4. Recall beats recognition, so multiple choice is a rung, not the goal.** Roediger & Karpicke (2006) found recall practice held 61% after a week against 40% for rereading. Build the ladder so a word only counts as mastered after free-recall and production successes. http://psychnet.wustl.edu/memory/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Roediger-Karpicke-2006_PPS.pdf

**5. Teach the most common usage plus one collocation, in three sentences, not a bare definition.** Webb found richer context drove meaning knowledge while raw repetition drove form knowledge, so the app needs both. Schmitt (2008) frames the whole thing as engagement: more contact of more kinds with each word. Schmitt, *Language Teaching Research* 12(3), 2008, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1362168808089921

**6. Use Arabic glosses early, then fade them.** Kim, Lee & Lee (2024) meta-analysed 78 effect sizes: L1 glosses beat L2 glosses overall, but the advantage sits on immediate tests and mostly for beginners. He is not a beginner. Show Arabic on tap, hidden by default, and drop it for a word once it reaches the recall rung. Sentence-level glosses beat single-word glosses (Kim et al., *System*, 2024). https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1362168820981394

**7. Teach word parts.** Bowers, Kirby & Deacon (2010) reviewed 22 studies and found morphological instruction helps literacy, with bigger gains for weaker readers. Group words by family (*decide / decision / decisive*) and make prefix and suffix work a recurring exercise. *Review of Educational Research* 80(2), https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654309359353

**8. Mix item types within a review block, but block first when a word is brand new.** Hwang et al. (2025) found interleaving hurt low-achieving adolescents until they had some blocked practice first. So: new word gets a blocked mini-set on day 1, then joins the interleaved pool. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lang.12659

**Defensible mastery criterion:** 4 correct answers across at least 3 different days and 3 different formats, including at least one production format (spell from audio, or use in a sentence), with the last correct answer at a gap of 7 days or more. Then a single 30-day re-check.

## 2. Reading

**9. Hold texts at 98% known words.** Hu & Nation (2000) put 95% at minimally adequate and 98% at the level where most readers actually understand unaided; Nation (2006) treats 98% as the threshold for picking up new words from reading. Kremmel et al. (2023) replicated it. At 98%, a 300-word passage carries about 6 unknown words, which is also a sensible daily vocabulary intake. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lang.12622

**10. Pair audio with text.** Chang & Millett (2015) found audio-assisted reading beat silent reading on both reading rate and comprehension for lower-level EFL learners. Give every passage a "listen while I read" mode and a "read alone" mode. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0346251X15000846

**11. Track fluency against real norms.** Hasbrouck & Tindal (2017), 50th percentile Grade 4: 94 WCPM in fall, 120 winter, 133 spring. Use repeated reading of the same short passage (2-3 reads) and chart words correct per minute. https://www.readingrockets.org/topics/fluency/articles/fluency-norms-chart-2017-update

**12. Ask about the text, do not drill strategy names.** McKeown, Beck & Blake (2009) compared strategy instruction with a content approach (Questioning the Author) and found the content approach equal or better. Use QtA queries during reading: "What is the author trying to say here?" "Does that fit with what he said before?" Then a retell. Dialogic reading and retelling show moderate effects (g ≈ 0.49-0.54). https://ila.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1598/RRQ.44.3.1

**13. Teach a small set of academic words hard, across days, inside one text.** That is recommendation 1 of the IES practice guide for English learners (Baker et al., 2014), which is the closest thing to an official evidence standard here. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/19

**14. Ramp difficulty on the 85% rule.** Wilson et al. (2019) put the optimal training accuracy near 85%. Grade 4 Lexile band is 740L-940L (MetaMetrics); start near 700L and step in 50L increments. Advance after 3 consecutive sessions at ≥85% comprehension accuracy and ≥95% word recognition. Drop a step after 2 sessions below 70%. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12552-4

## 3. Motivation

**15. Reward effort and visible mastery, not correctness.** A meta-analysis of 35 gamified interventions found a small positive effect, mostly on autonomy and relatedness rather than competence (Huang et al., *ETR&D*, 2023). https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7

**16. Keep streaks, drop hearts.** Hadi Mogavi et al. (2022) studied nine years of Duolingo forum posts and 15 interviews and named "gamification misuse," where competitiveness and game mechanics pull attention off the learning. Lives systems punish the exact hard items that produce learning, and easy wins farmed for points teach nothing. https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16175

Practical rules: immediate feedback with the correct answer shown, a visible word map showing which words are at which rung, streak counted by session completed rather than accuracy, no leaderboard, no lives, no timed penalty.

## 4. Daily session recipe (25 minutes)

1. **Review, 5 min.** All due items, interleaved across word rungs and skills. Expect 20-30 items.
2. **New words, 8 min.** 3 words. Each gets audio, a picture, 3 example sentences showing the most common usage, one collocation, and an Arabic gloss behind a tap. Blocked mini-drill of those 3 before moving on.
3. **Reading, 8 min.** 250-400 words at 98% coverage, read-while-listening on new texts, silent reread on day 2. Two QtA prompts during reading, one inference question, one oral retell.
4. **Production, 4 min.** Spell 3 words from audio, or complete a cloze, or write one sentence using a target word.

Mastery ladder for one word: **recognise** (meaning from 4 options) → **listen** (hear it, pick or type it) → **recall** (see word, produce meaning) → **spell** (from audio) → **use** (cloze or own sentence). One correct answer promotes a rung; a miss drops it one rung and resets the gap to 1 day.

## Defaults for the app

| Setting | Default | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Session length | 25 min, 6 days/week | practical |
| New words per session | 3 (15/week, ~180 in 12 weeks) | Beck, McKeown & Kucan: 8-10/week taught robustly; doubled here because sessions are 1:1 and daily |
| Encounters before "known" | 10 spaced encounters | Webb 2007 |
| Review gaps (days) | 1, 3, 7, 16, 35, then 90 | Cepeda 2006 (gap ≈ 10-20% of retention target); expanding per Nakata 2015 |
| Mastery rule | 4 correct, ≥3 days, ≥3 formats, ≥1 productive, last gap ≥7 days | Karpicke & Roediger 2008 |
| Lapse rule | Miss = drop one rung, gap resets to 1 day | standard spaced-repetition practice |
| Text coverage | 98% known words (~6 unknown per 300) | Hu & Nation 2000; Nation 2006 |
| Passage length | 250-400 words, +50 words per level step | Lexile grade band |
| Starting difficulty | 700L, steps of 50L, ceiling 940L | MetaMetrics Grade 4 band 740-940L |
| Level up | 3 consecutive sessions ≥85% comprehension and ≥95% word recognition | Wilson et al. 2019 |
| Level down | 2 consecutive sessions <70% comprehension | Wilson et al. 2019 |
| Target session accuracy | 85% | Wilson et al. 2019 |
| Fluency goal | 94 → 133 WCPM over the 12 weeks | Hasbrouck & Tindal 2017 |
| Repeated reading | Same passage 2-3 times, chart WCPM weekly | Chang & Millett 2015 |
| Arabic gloss | On tap, hidden by default, off once word reaches "recall" | Kim, Lee & Lee 2024 |
| Comprehension questions | 2 QtA prompts + 1 inference + 1 retell per passage | McKeown, Beck & Blake 2009 |
| Gamification | Streak on completion, XP on effort, mastery map visible. No lives, no leaderboard, no timer | Huang et al. 2023; Hadi Mogavi et al. 2022 |

One honest caveat on the 3-month goal. 180 explicitly taught words will not by itself close a full grade level, since typical children add roughly 3,000 words a year mostly from reading. The explicit list is the high-value core; the daily reading at 98% coverage is what carries the rest. Comprehension and fluency should move faster than raw vocabulary size, and those are what a Grade 4 assessment actually measures.
